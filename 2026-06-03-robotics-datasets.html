My previous blog post takes a data-centric view of end-to-end robot learning. I have also written about open-source vision datasets.

This post compares the scale and source of datasets used in robot learning. It includes public datasets, disclosed private corpora, and training mixtures described in papers and company posts.

Conversion Rules¶

  • Use reported hours when they exist.
  • Otherwise, assume one robot trajectory is 15 seconds: 240 trajectories = 1 hour.
  • For robot frames or steps, use the reported rate. If it is missing, assume 10 Hz: 36,000 steps = 1 hour.
  • For video clips, assume 10 seconds: 360 clips = 1 hour.
  • For still images, use one second per image: 3,600 images = one image-equivalent hour.
  • Count synchronized camera views as one timeline.
  • Round aggressively. ~ means converted rather than reported.
  • Raw source pools, model training mixes, and released datasets are different. The notes say which one a row represents.
  • Notes mark known reuse or known disjointness. private and self-collected do not by themselves prove no overlap.

The 15-second rule sits near AgiBot World (10.7 seconds/trajectory), RT-1 (14.5), DROID (16.6), BC-Z (17.4), and FastUMI-100K (21.6).

Rows named training mix come from model papers. They often reuse the named public datasets and should not be added together.
